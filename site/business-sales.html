Selling Your Flooring Business, Done Properly

Most owners sell a business once. There's no practice run, and the parts that cost people money are rarely the parts they were worried about. I'll tell you what the business is really worth, what's holding that number down, and take it to the right buyers without your staff, suppliers or competitors finding out.

What Quietly Takes Money Off the Price

These are the things a buyer's accountant will find. It's far cheaper to find them first.

Owner dependency

If the key customer relationships, the pricing and the know-how all sit with you, a buyer is paying for something that walks out of the door on completion day.

Customer concentration

How much of your turnover sits with your largest few accounts, and whether those relationships belong to the business or to one person.

Undocumented agreements

Supplier terms, rebates, exclusivity and distribution arrangements agreed on a handshake. A buyer will assume they disappear unless you can show otherwise.

Stock and working capital

Slow-moving stock, how it's valued, and what finance sits behind the business. All of it changes the cash you actually walk away with.

How your fitters are engaged

Self-employed and subcontract arrangements that wouldn't stand up to HMRC are a liability a buyer will price in, or walk away from.

Add-backs that don't survive

Adjusted profit is where valuations are won and lost. I test every add-back the way a buyer would, before a buyer does.

Importer, distributor or wholesaler? The valuation conversation is a different one: agency agreements, stock ageing, currency and finance facilities matter most. I'll cover those with you.

Confidentiality

Everything you share is treated in the strictest confidence and used only for this work. Nothing goes to market without your sign-off, and the business can't be identified in anything I circulate until a buyer has signed a non-disclosure agreement.

It's worth thinking about at your end too. Early on, I'd suggest this stays between you, your accountant and anyone whose agreement you need. Staff and suppliers work things out faster than most owners expect.

What this is not

I'm not a tax adviser or a financial adviser. How a sale is structured and how the proceeds are taxed will affect what you keep, so bring your accountant in early. A valuation is a professional opinion based on the information provided and the market at the time. It isn't a guarantee of what a buyer will pay.
