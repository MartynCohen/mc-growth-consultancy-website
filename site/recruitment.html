Why Flooring Roles Are Hard to Fill

The usual route is an advert, a stack of applications and a recruiter who has never stood in a flooring showroom or walked a contract site. You do the sifting, the best candidate takes a counter-offer, and three months later you're back where you started.

Meanwhile the role stays empty, or worse, gets filled by someone who was simply available. In a business your size, one wrong hire in a key seat costs far more than the salary.

I've hired, led and developed flooring sales teams, operations teams and installer networks for most of my career. I know what good looks like in each of these jobs, and I know the difference between someone who interviews well and someone who'll still be performing in a year.

What I Do Instead

  • Take a proper brief: the role, the business, and what success looks like twelve months in.
  • Write or tidy the job description with you, so you're hiring for the job you actually need done.
  • Search the market and approach people directly, including those who aren't looking.
  • Use referrals from people in the trade whose judgement I trust.
  • Interview every candidate myself before you see a CV.
  • Manage the offer, the notice period and the counter-offer conversation.
  • Help your new starter settle, with an induction plan if you want one.

How a Search Runs

The same five steps every time. How long each takes depends on the role and how many people in the country can do it.

  1. 1
    The briefWe talk through the role, the team, the pay and the kind of person who'll fit. I'll challenge anything that looks unrealistic, because it's better to hear that from me now than from the market later.
  2. 2
    The searchI map who's doing this job well for other businesses and approach them directly. Your name stays out of it until someone is seriously interested, if that's how you want it.
  3. 3
    The shortlistI interview everyone first. You get a small number of people with my honest notes on each: strengths, gaps, what they want and what it'll take to get them.
  4. 4
    Interviews and offerYou meet the shortlist. I gather feedback both ways, handle the offer and stay close while they hand their notice in, which is when good people get counter-offered.
  5. 5
    Start and settleOn the end-to-end service I help plan the induction and spend time with your new starter, so the first few weeks build the right habits.

Fitters and Installers

Finding fitters is a different job to finding a sales rep. There are fewer of them every year, the good ones are booked up, and how you engage them matters as much as how many you have.

I've overseen a network of more than 1,300 self-employed fitters, so I can help with both sides: sourcing and vetting installers at scale, and setting up the agreements, standards and paperwork that keep HMRC and your customers happy.

How I Charge

No numbers on a web page, because the right fee depends on the role. Here's how it works.

You pay when someone starts

For permanent roles the fee is a percentage of the first year's salary, agreed in writing before I begin. Nothing is due until your new hire starts.

If it doesn't work out

If your new hire leaves in the first three months, I look for a replacement first. If I can't find one, a sliding refund applies. It's all in the terms you see upfront.

Plain terms, no surprises

You get short, readable terms of business before I start. Sales agent introductions and installer sourcing are priced differently and I'll explain how when we talk.
